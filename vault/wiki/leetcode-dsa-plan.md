## Role & language targeting

- **Target**: Tier-2 Backend/AI engineering roles, technical interview
  prep. "Tier-2" here means solid product/services companies and mid-size
  AI-adjacent shops — not top-of-market FAANG-style loops that lean
  disproportionately on Hard-tier DP/graph puzzles. Expect a **coding
  round anchored in Medium-difficulty patterns**, decent weight on
  practical structures (hashing, trees, heaps, intervals — things that
  map directly to caching, request-scheduling, and search/ranking
  problems backend/AI teams actually hit), and comparatively less
  emphasis on obscure Hard-tier DP or bit-manipulation trivia.
- **Language**: Python throughout. Lean on idiomatic constructs
  interviewers expect fluency in — `collections.Counter`,
  `collections.deque`, `heapq`, dict/set comprehensions, tuple unpacking
  for multi-return, and being able to state Python-specific complexity
  gotchas (e.g. list `.pop(0)` is O(n), `in` on a list is O(n) vs O(1) on
  a set/dict).

## How to use this plan

- **Time per day**: budget ~1-2 hours/day (part-time study). Each non-review
  day's 2-4 problems fit that window (roughly 20-30 min/problem including
  reading the concept notes and reviewing the solution afterward).
  - Suggested breakdown: ~10-15 min re-reading the day's concept notes,
    ~60-90 min actually solving problems (timer on, editorial only after a
    genuine attempt), ~10-15 min writing a 2-3 line note per problem
    (pattern used, what you missed) into your mistake log in `vault/raw/`.
- **Order matters**: solve problems in the listed order — each day builds on
  the previous one, and Weeks 2-4 lean on patterns from Week 1 (queues reuse
  arrays, graphs reuse BFS/DFS, DP reuses recursion).
- **Don't skip rest/review days** (Days 6, 14, 21, 28): they're built-in
  spaced-repetition checkpoints — re-solving 2-3 older problems *cold* (no
  notes) is what actually moves patterns into long-term memory, not just
  seeing them once.
- **Quiz checkpoints**: run `mentor-quiz-master` after Days 7, 14, 21, and 28
  to test retention on that week's patterns before moving on — results and
  any weak topics land in `vault/raw/` for the Synthesizer's Log
  Parser to pick up.
- **If a problem stumps you for >30 min**: read the editorial/hints, but
  re-solve it from scratch 1-2 days later without looking — struggling
  first, then reviewing, beats reading a solution cold.
- **Mock-assessment days**: Days 7, 21, and 29 are timed, notes-closed mock
  rounds (3 total, per the mock-assessment requirement) — treat them like
  the real thing, narrate your approach out loud, and log every miss.

## Weekly themes

| Week | Theme | Days |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations: arrays, two pointers, sliding window, hashing | 1-7 |
| 2 | Linear structures: stacks/queues, linked lists, binary search, backtracking intro | 8-14 |
| 3 | Nonlinear structures: trees, heaps, graphs, union-find | 15-21 |
| 4 | Advanced: DP, greedy, intervals, bit manipulation, tries | 22-28 |
| 5 | Mock interviews + final review | 29-30 |

Spaced repetition: each rest/review day (6, 14, 21, 28) requires re-solving
2-3 problems **cold** (no notes) from a prior week before looking at new
material. Day 30 is a full-syllabus review, not just Week 4.

## Day-by-day plan

### Week 1 — Foundations: arrays, strings, two pointers, sliding window, hashing

- **Day 1 — Arrays & Strings I**
  - Concepts: array indexing/iteration basics; Big-O time/space complexity;
    in-place vs. extra-space tradeoffs
  - Problems (Easy x4): Two Sum, Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock, Contains
    Duplicate, Valid Anagram
- **Day 2 — Arrays & Strings II**
  - Concepts: prefix sums; Kadane's algorithm (running max); in-place array
    mutation
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Maximum Subarray, Product of Array Except
    Self (Medium), Rotate Array (Medium), Move Zeroes (Easy)
- **Day 3 — Two Pointers**
  - Concepts: two-pointer technique on sorted data; when two pointers beat
    an O(n²) nested loop
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Valid Palindrome (Easy), Two Sum II - Input
    Array Is Sorted (Medium), 3Sum (Medium), Container With Most Water
    (Medium)
- **Day 4 — Sliding Window**
  - Concepts: fixed-size vs. variable-size window; expand/shrink invariant
  - Problems (Medium x4): Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters,
    Minimum Size Subarray Sum, Longest Repeating Character Replacement,
    Permutation in String
- **Day 5 — Hashing**
  - Concepts: hash map/set O(1)-average lookup; frequency counting;
    grouping by derived key; Python's `Counter`/`defaultdict`
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Group Anagrams (Medium), Top K Frequent
    Elements (Medium), Longest Consecutive Sequence (Medium), Valid Sudoku
    (Medium)
- **Day 6 — Rest/Review**
  - Concepts: retrieval practice / spaced repetition
  - Re-solve 2 problems from Days 1-5 cold (no notes); log mistakes to
    `vault/raw/`
- **Day 7 — Mini mock (timed, 90 min) — mock assessment #1**
  - Concepts: solving under time pressure; narrating your approach out loud
  - 3 mixed problems drawn from arrays/two pointers/sliding window

### Week 2 — Linear structures & search

- **Day 8 — Stacks**
  - Concepts: LIFO ordering; stacks for matching/parsing; intro to monotonic
    stack
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Valid Parentheses (Easy), Min Stack
    (Medium), Evaluate Reverse Polish Notation (Medium), Daily Temperatures
    (Medium)
- **Day 9 — Queues & monotonic structures**
  - Concepts: FIFO ordering; monotonic deque for sliding-window maximum;
    Python `collections.deque` (O(1) append/pop from both ends vs. list)
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x3): Implement Queue using Stacks (Easy), Sliding
    Window Maximum (Medium/Hard — attempt it, it's a standard pattern),
    Next Greater Element I (Easy)
- **Day 10 — Linked Lists I**
  - Concepts: pointer manipulation; dummy-node technique; fast/slow pointer
    cycle detection
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Reverse Linked List (Easy), Merge Two Sorted
    Lists (Easy), Linked List Cycle (Easy), Reorder List (Medium)
- **Day 11 — Linked Lists II**
  - Concepts: carry propagation across nodes; multi-pointer traversal; deep
    copy using a hash map
  - Problems (Medium x3): Add Two Numbers, Remove Nth Node From End of
    List, Copy List with Random Pointer
- **Day 12 — Binary Search**
  - Concepts: search-space halving; binary search on a rotated array;
    "search on the answer" pattern
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Binary Search (Easy), Search in Rotated
    Sorted Array (Medium), Find Minimum in Rotated Sorted Array (Medium),
    Koko Eating Bananas (Medium)
- **Day 13 — Recursion & Backtracking intro**
  - Concepts: base case vs. recursive case; backtrack-and-undo pattern;
    decision tree of choices
  - Problems (Medium x3): Subsets, Permutations, Combination Sum
- **Day 14 — Rest/Review**
  - Re-solve 2-3 problems from Week 1 cold; review Week 2 notes

### Week 3 — Trees, heaps, graphs

- **Day 15 — Binary Trees I**
  - Concepts: recursion on trees; DFS traversal; recursive height/depth
    computation
  - Problems (Easy x4): Invert Binary Tree, Maximum Depth of Binary Tree,
    Diameter of Binary Tree, Balanced Binary Tree
- **Day 16 — BST**
  - Concepts: BST invariant (left < node < right); in-order traversal
    yields sorted order
  - Problems (Medium x3): Validate Binary Search Tree, Lowest Common
    Ancestor of a BST, Kth Smallest Element in a BST
- **Day 17 — Traversals & construction**
  - Concepts: BFS level-order traversal via a queue; serialize/deserialize;
    rebuilding a tree from traversal orders
  - Problems (Medium-Hard x3): Binary Tree Level Order Traversal (Medium),
    Serialize and Deserialize Binary Tree (Hard), Construct Binary Tree
    from Preorder and Inorder Traversal (Medium)
- **Day 18 — Heaps**
  - Concepts: min-heap/max-heap operations; top-K pattern; heap for
    streaming data; Python `heapq` (min-heap only — negate for max-heap)
  - Problems (Medium-Hard x4): Kth Largest Element in an Array (Medium),
    Merge k Sorted Lists (Hard), Top K Frequent Words (Medium), Find Median
    from Data Stream (Hard)
- **Day 19 — Graphs BFS/DFS**
  - Concepts: adjacency-list representation; BFS vs. DFS traversal;
    visited-set to avoid revisiting/cycles
  - Problems (Medium x4): Number of Islands, Clone Graph, Course Schedule,
    Pacific Atlantic Water Flow
- **Day 20 — Union-Find & more graphs**
  - Concepts: disjoint-set union/find with path compression; cycle
    detection via union-find
  - Problems (Medium x3): Number of Connected Components in an Undirected
    Graph, Redundant Connection, Graph Valid Tree
- **Day 21 — Mock contest (timed, 90 min) — mock assessment #2**
  - 3 mixed problems from trees/heaps/graphs + review your weakest topic
    from Days 15-20

### Week 4 — Dynamic programming, greedy, misc advanced

- **Day 22 — DP I (1D)**
  - Concepts: overlapping subproblems; memoization vs. tabulation; state
    transition indexed on a single dimension
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Climbing Stairs (Easy), House Robber
    (Medium), Coin Change (Medium), Longest Increasing Subsequence (Medium)
- **Day 23 — DP II (2D/strings)**
  - Concepts: 2D DP table; string-alignment/edit-operation transitions
  - Problems (Medium x4): Longest Common Subsequence, Edit Distance, Unique
    Paths, Word Break
- **Day 24 — DP III (subsequence/knapsack) + spaced repetition**
  - Concepts: expand-around-center vs. DP for palindromes; 0/1-knapsack
    framing of subset-sum problems
  - Problems (Medium x3) + 2 cold re-solves from Week 2: Longest
    Palindromic Substring, Partition Equal Subset Sum, Target Sum
- **Day 25 — Greedy**
  - Concepts: locally-optimal choice + exchange-argument intuition; when
    greedy is provably correct vs. a trap
  - Problems (Medium x4): Jump Game, Gas Station, Task Scheduler, Merge
    Intervals
- **Day 26 — Intervals & Bit Manipulation**
  - Concepts: interval sort-then-merge pattern; XOR properties; basic
    bitmasking
  - Problems (Easy-Medium x4): Insert Interval (Medium), Non-overlapping
    Intervals (Medium), Single Number (Easy), Number of 1 Bits (Easy)
- **Day 27 — Tries & misc**
  - Concepts: trie node structure; prefix search; combining DFS + trie for
    word search
  - Problems (Medium-Hard x3): Implement Trie (Prefix Tree) (Medium),
    Design Add and Search Words Data Structure (Medium), Word Search II
    (Hard)
- **Day 28 — Rest/Review**
  - Full spaced-repetition pass: re-solve 3 previously-missed problems
    spanning at least 3 different weeks

### Week 5 — Mock interviews & final review

- **Day 29 — Full mock interview (timed, 45-60 min) — mock assessment #3**
  - Concepts: performing under realistic interview conditions; talking
    through your reasoning while coding
  - 1 medium + 1 hard problem, no notes, plus a short behavioral/
    communication run-through
- **Day 30 — Final review**
  - Revisit the mistake log built across all review days, re-solve the 3-5
    hardest problems from the month, and decide next steps

## Progress measurement — what "done" looks like per week

- **Week 1 done**: all 5 daily problem sets solved (notes allowed on first
  attempt); Day 6 cold re-solves succeed without hints; Day 7 mock — solve
  at least 2/3 problems within the 90-minute window and correctly state
  time/space complexity for each out loud.
- **Week 2 done**: comfortable writing a monotonic stack/queue from
  scratch; can reverse/manipulate a linked list with a dummy node without
  referencing notes; binary search "on the answer" (e.g. Koko Eating
  Bananas) solved without seeing it as a new pattern each time; Day 14 cold
  re-solves from Week 1 succeed.
- **Week 3 done**: can write recursive tree DFS and iterative BFS
  (level-order via queue) from memory; heap usage for top-K is automatic,
  not derived each time; graph traversal (BFS/DFS + visited-set) solved
  without hints on at least 3/4 Day 19 problems; Day 21 mock — 2/3
  problems solved, weakest topic from Days 15-20 identified and named.
- **Week 4 done**: can identify "this is a DP problem" from problem
  phrasing and define the state/transition before coding; greedy vs.
  DP/exhaustive-search distinction can be articulated with a concrete
  counterexample; Day 28 full spaced-repetition pass clears with fewer
  hints than the equivalent problems took in their original week.
- **Week 5 / overall done**: Day 29 mock completed under real time
  pressure with reasoning narrated throughout (not just a working
  solution); mistake log in `vault/raw/` reviewed end-to-end on Day 30 with
  every previously-missed problem re-attempted at least once successfully;
  self-rated ability to solve a cold Medium-difficulty problem from any of
  the 17 syllabus topics within 25-30 minutes.
- **Quiz checkpoints** (Days 7, 14, 21, 28 via `mentor-quiz-master`) are the
  objective backstop for the above — any topic flagged weak there
  overrides self-assessment and should be re-drilled before moving on.

## After Day 30

- **If most patterns feel solid**: move to harder problems (Hard-tagged
  LeetCode, or a "top 150/blind 75"-style curated hard set) and start timed
  full mock interviews 2-3x/week.
- **If specific topics are still shaky** (check the weak-topic list in
  `vault/raw/` from quiz checkpoints): don't move on — spend 3-5 more days
  drilling just that topic before advancing, using
  `mentor-concept-explainer` for a fresh explanation and
  `mentor-quiz-master` to re-check retention.
- **System design**: once comfortable with DSA, [[system-design]] prep is
  the natural next module for senior/staff-track roles — ask Mentor for a
  new `mentor-curriculum` pass targeting that goal. For Tier-2 Backend/AI
  roles specifically, this is high-value even at mid-level, since system
  design rounds are common there earlier in the ladder than at
  pure-FAANG tracks.
- **Keep a running mistake log**: the single highest-leverage habit after
  Day 30 is continuing to log every missed problem (pattern, why you
  missed it, correct approach) to `vault/raw/` — that's what future
  `mentor-quiz-master` and Synthesizer passes draw on.

## Recommended structure notes

- **Weekly themes**: each week is a dependency layer — don't jump to graphs
  before hashing/BFS-DFS fundamentals are solid, since graph traversal
  reuses the queue/stack/recursion patterns from Weeks 1-2.
- **Spaced repetition**: rest days (6, 14, 21, 28) are not optional skips —
  they're built-in retrieval practice on earlier weeks' patterns.
- **Mock interview week**: Days 29-30 intentionally mix old + new material
  under time pressure to simulate the real interview rather than
  topic-isolated practice.
- **Quiz checkpoints**: run `mentor-quiz-master` after Days 7, 14, 21, and
  28 to test retention on that week's patterns before moving on — results
  and any weak topics should land in `vault/raw/` for the
  Synthesizer's Log Parser to pick up.
